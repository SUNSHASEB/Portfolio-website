01 · Overview

A little structure. Endless possibility.

It started as a Figma sketch of a hardware sequencer. Now it plays: a responsive four-module workspace with circular step pads, eight faders and an A/B effects crossfader.

  1. 01Sequence

    Eight patterns, eight tracks, sixteen steps. Velocity, probability and note per step.

  2. 02Sound

    Drum and synth engines, or load your own sample.

  3. 03Mix

    Eight faders, mute, solo and a master level.

  4. 04Perform

    Two effect scenes and a crossfader between them.

02 · Where it started

It started with hardware.

Before any code, a Figma board of the boxes I love to play. Then sketches, screens and layouts, until the idea was ready to build.

Figma note, day one

“The idea is to create a software plugin inspired by the Faderfox MIDI controller. To use as a looper / groovebox, something like the Octatrack MK2.”

Twelve screen ideas

Little LED screens, drawn before a single sound existed. Tap one to switch it on.

From sketch to screen

A hardware box called BIT///001: LED screen, step grid, a fader and toggle switches.

03 · Try it

Tap a step. Press play.

A tiny version of the sequencer, running right here. The real one has eight tracks, eight patterns and a lot more knobs.

BIT///Mini sequencer
A · CleanB · Dub space

Tap a step, press play. Tap a pink track to mute it. Slide to B for filter and echo.

04 · The screen

Six ways to watch the beat.

The display is a playground. Touch or drag it to play notes: across for pitch, up and down for velocity.

The Figma signal-flow drawing, lit by the audio.

05 · Sound

Shape it by dragging.

Each voice has its own engine. Decay, filter, pitch and pan are curves you drag, and the sliders follow.

  1. 01Eight engines

    Analog kick, noise snare, hats, clap, bass, synth and bell.

  2. 02Your samples

    Drop in a WAV, MP3 or OGG up to 20 MB.

  3. 03Audition

    Hear every change before it hits the pattern.

Drag to shorten or stretch the hit.

06 · Mix & perform

Eight faders. Two scenes.

Every track gets a fader, mute and solo. Each scene picks its own effect, amount and beat rate.

  • Clean
  • Dub space
  • Low-pass sweep
  • High-pass sweep
  • Beat echo
  • Warehouse reverb
  • Jet flanger
  • Rhythmic tremolo
  • Saturation
07 · Hardware

Hands on, with a Faderfox LV3.

Plug in the controller and BIT maps itself. Faders, steps and transport all live on real buttons.

  1. 01Auto-map

    Eight faders set volume; black buttons select tracks.

  2. 02Step rows

    Green and blue rows toggle all sixteen steps, with LED feedback.

  3. 03Transport

    Push the top encoders to stop, play and pause.

08 · Light & dark

Ivory shell, dark screen.

Light mode swaps to ivory with graphite labels and pastel accents. The display always stays dark.

  1. 01Three workspaces

    Studio, Perform and Focus. Hide or reorder any module.

  2. 02Fits the window

    On desktop the whole instrument scales to fit, no scrolling.

09 · Under the hood

Built to keep time.

No libraries, no build step. Just the browser’s own audio and MIDI.

  1. 01Tight timing

    A 25 ms look-ahead scheduler on the audio clock.

  2. 02Export

    Bounce four bars to a stereo 44.1 kHz WAV.

  3. 03Saves itself

    Projects and samples stay in the browser, or save a portable file.

  4. 04Tested

    Unit tests plus offline checks for every engine and effect.

Your sound. Your system.
