Good Game Iraq: a neon brand and a mobile-first tournament platform for Iraq's competitive gamers.

01The brief

Iraq's arena. No identity.

A big platform for Iraqi gamers. No visual voice yet.

02Tone

Loud, but never generic.

Before opening Figma, I wrote the creative brief: audience, voice, key messages and references. These rules came out of it.

03Creative direction

Two worlds, one arena.

04The logo

Same mark. New voltage.

GGI already had a mark. I kept its geometry and gave it a new life: first in gold, then as neon, so it could glow on a dark city screen.

05Key visual

Where champions are forged.

Four directions, from heritage gold to neon city. The neon city won: it felt like a place gamers would want to enter.

06Brand system

Signal, not noise.

A near-black base with blue undertones. Neon used sparingly, as a signal. Sharp, modular parts that feel like a game HUD.

Dark Base#0A0F1E
Neon Accent#00E5FF
Cool Blue#0EA5E9
07The platform

Built for the phone.

Every element is a reusable Figma component, so the team can launch new tournaments, games and seasons without starting over.

  1. 01Mobile-first

    Tested at 375px first. What works on a phone earns its place on desktop.

  2. 02Hierarchy first

    Drama comes from structure and contrast, not added effects.

  3. 03Ten-second cards

    Game, dates, mode, prize and one button. Nothing else.

  4. 04Sponsors stay visible

    Sponsor logos get their own layer, always readable.

  5. 05Templates, not tickets

    Fixed image ratios and banner templates keep uploads on-brand.

  6. 06Proud, not literal

    Iraqi identity lives in atmosphere and tone, not clichés.

08Social

Two languages, one voice.

Posts and stories in English and Arabic, built from the same key visual.

09Reflection

The brief is the deliverable.
