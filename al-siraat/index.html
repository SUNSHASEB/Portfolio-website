01 Context

Read less. Learn more.

Most Quran apps help you read. Very few help you learn.

I joined as Head of Design to lead the team behind 9D Muslim Pro, with a mandate to reimagine it as an AI Quran learning app.

Two audiences: first-time readers starting from the Arabic alphabet, and readers who want better recitation and Tajweed. Both needed the instant feedback of a live tutor.

03 Research

Learning from the AI-Quran space.

I studied Tarteel, Qara'a and Ngaji.ai / Koran.AI. Three patterns stood out:

AI feedback wins

Recite, get corrected, improve. That loop brings people back daily.

Beginners need an on-ramp

Apps that started at full recitation lost first-time learners. An alphabet-first Qaida track was essential.

Progress drives habit

Streaks, mistake logs and trackers turn learning into a routine.

So we sequenced the roadmap around the beginner's journey:

  1. P1Qaida AI learning + core worship tools
  2. P2The Quran viewer
  3. P3Full Quran recitation AI
04 Process · 9D JAM

Design together. Decide fast.

A team design sprint: map old modules to the new structure, draw the flows, then sketches → wireframes → high fidelity.

05 Four directions

Options, side by side.

Each designer explored one direction for the new home. Seeing them together made the trade-offs obvious.

Clean, card-based, green accent.

Warm gold, quick-action driven.

Photographic, soft gradient cards.

Deep green, mosque imagery, tool-first.

06 The breakthrough

"Salam! Learn with AI."

We stopped treating home as a tool launcher. Qaida and Quran learning became the two hero cards; worship tools moved to a supporting layer. A dozen layouts later, it clicked.

08 Brand identity

Al Siraat. The straight path.

Not a collection of tools. Guidance along a path.

A product this different couldn't ship under the old name. The name comes from Siraat al-Mustaqeem, the straight path asked for in daily prayer.

I designed the logo and identity around it, "Connecting Hearts, Enlightening Hearts". It runs across the app icon, onboarding, alsiraat.ai and social.

09 What we shipped

A personal AI tutor. 24/7.

Launched on a freemium model and still shipping: audio while learning, Qaida articulations, Ramadan theming, home-screen widgets and more.

10 Reflections

What it taught me.

Subtraction is a strategy.

The biggest decision wasn't adding AI. It was demoting a dozen features so two learning journeys could lead.

Prioritisation is a deliverable.

The P1 / P2 / P3 module structure aligned design, engineering and business, and the shipped product followed it.

Options beat opinions.

Four dashboard directions and three full style systems turned debates into structured decisions.

A rename is a product decision.

"Al Siraat, the straight path" states the product's promise in two words.

"I'm loving the whole app. I would recommend this to every Muslim trying to learn Quranic Arabic."
