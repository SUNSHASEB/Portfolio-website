01 The brief

Iraq's arena. No identity.

10sTo see the game, the prize and how to join
375pxEvery layout designed here first
2Languages, English & Arabic
2Months, brief to hand-off

A big platform for Iraqi gamers. No visual voice yet.

Estaz Esports asked me to bring creative clarity to GGI, a tournament platform for Iraq's fast-growing gaming scene.

The core user is mobile-first and lives on Discord. In ten seconds they need to know: which game, is the prize real, and how do I sign up.

02 Tone

Loud, but never generic.

Before opening Figma, I wrote the creative brief: audience, voice, key messages and references. These rules came out of it.

Do
  • Competitive, high-energy, confident
  • Community-driven, Discord-first
  • Premium, not over-polished
  • Subtle Iraqi cues
Don't
  • Generic esports templates
  • Neon for the sake of neon
  • Cultural stereotypes
  • Flash over readability

04 The logo

Same mark. New voltage.

GGI already had a mark. I kept its geometry and gave it a new life: first in gold, then as neon, so it could glow on a dark city screen.

The mark GGI already had: two G shapes locked into a diamond.

Exploration: the same mark cast in gold, for trophies and podiums.

Final: the same geometry, redrawn as a neon sign that lights up the city.

05 Key visual

Where champions are forged.

Four directions, from heritage gold to neon city. The neon city won: it felt like a place gamers would want to enter.

An arena tunnel in purple and gold.

Golden arches: heritage first.

A glowing mark on deep navy.

A neon city with a holographic mark. The winner.

The final key visual: "Where champions are forged."

06 Brand system

Signal, not noise.

A near-black base with blue undertones. Neon used sparingly, as a signal. Sharp, modular parts that feel like a game HUD.

07 The platform

Built for the phone.

Every element is a reusable Figma component, so the team can launch new tournaments, games and seasons without starting over.

Headers, menus and tournament cards.

Profile, notifications, rank and history.

Homepage, listings and detail pages.

Event banners and winner podiums.

01Mobile-first

Tested at 375px first. What works on a phone earns its place on desktop.

02Hierarchy first

Drama comes from structure and contrast, not added effects.

03Ten-second cards

Game, dates, mode, prize and one button. Nothing else.

04Sponsors stay visible

Sponsor logos get their own layer, always readable.

05Templates, not tickets

Fixed image ratios and banner templates keep uploads on-brand.

06Proud, not literal

Iraqi identity lives in atmosphere and tone, not clichés.

08 Social

Two languages, one voice.

Posts and stories in English and Arabic, built from the same key visual.

09 Reflection

The brief is the deliverable.

Brief before pixels

Agreeing who it's for and what it must never do made every visual call easier.

Evolve, don't erase

Keeping the original mark's geometry gave GGI a fresh look without losing recognition.

Systems scale seasons

Components and templates let a small team ship new events fast.
